## Third Shot Drive or Drop? The Three-Read Checklist

Stop coin-flipping your third shot. Three things to read before the ball gets to you, and a rule for what to do when they disagree.

4.7 DUPR Doctor of Chiropractic PCI Certified Coach

By [Dr. Lane Odom, DC](https://pickleballchiro.co/about/) · PCI Certified Coach · Published September 14, 2026 · 8 min read

The third shot in pickleball should be a drive or a drop based on three reads: how deep the return is, how high it bounces, and whether your opponents are on the kitchen line or off it. Deep, high, and opponents off the line leans drive. Short, low, and opponents already at the line leans drop. When the reads disagree, two out of three decides.

This is the second article in the [Why You’re Stuck at 3.5](https://pickleballchiro.co/why-youre-stuck-at-3-5/) series. That one covered the five leaks. This one goes deep on the first decision inside Leak 1, because in the game film I review, the third shot is the first thing I check, and it’s the decision intermediate players most often make with no rule at all.

If you’re a 3.5 trying to get to 4.0, you don’t need a better drive or a better drop. You need to stop guessing which one to hit. That’s what this checklist is for.

### Why 3.5 Players Coin-Flip the Third Shot

Most intermediate players hit the third shot they like, not the one the ball asked for. Drivers drive everything and drop when they’re scared. Droppers drop everything and drive when they’re frustrated. Neither one is reading the ball, so neither one gets better at the decision, no matter how many they hit.

#### How it shows up

Your drive sails long or gets volleyed back at your feet because you hit it off a low ball with two people already at the line. Or your drop sits up in the kitchen because you tried to feather a deep, shoulder-high return with your feet still moving. Same player, both errors, same game. The shot wasn’t the problem. The choice was.

#### Why it happens

Nobody gave you a rule. Coaches say “drop when they’re at the line, drive when they’re not,” which is one read out of three, and it’s the read you have the least time to make. So you fall back on preference. The fix is not a new shot. It’s three questions you answer in the half second before contact, every single time, until you don’t have to think about them.

### Read 1: How Deep Is the Ball?

Depth is where the return lands relative to your baseline. A deep return that pushes you back behind the line leans drive. A short return that lets you step in and take the ball inside the baseline leans drop, because you’re closer to the kitchen and the drop is a shorter, easier shot.

#### What to look for

Watch the return come off their paddle and ask one thing: am I moving backward or forward to get to it? Backward means deep. Forward means short. That’s the whole read. You don’t need to judge feet and inches.

#### Why it matters

This is the read most players get backwards. They think a short ball is a gift to drive. But a short ball puts you closer to the net, and closer to the net is exactly where a drop becomes easy and a drive becomes a pop-up waiting to happen. A deep ball is the opposite: you’re far from the kitchen, a drop has to travel a long way and stay low the whole trip, and a drive off a deep ball can be hit with margin and still be hard to handle.

### Read 2: How High Is the Ball?

Height is where the ball is when you make contact, relative to the net. A ball you contact above net height leans drive, because you can hit down or flat on it. A ball you contact below net height leans drop, because driving it means lifting it, and a lifted drive is the easiest ball in pickleball to counter.

#### What to look for

Where is your paddle at contact: above your waist or below your knee? Waist and up is a high ball. Knee and down is a low ball. Between is a judgment call, which is why you need the other two reads.

#### Why it matters

The net is 34 inches in the middle. If you contact the ball below that, a drive has to go up before it goes down, and a player at the line will block it back at your feet. Intermediate players drive low balls constantly because the ball “felt” hittable. It was hittable. It just wasn’t driveable. Below the net tape, the drop is almost always the higher-percentage shot.

### Read 3: Are They on the Line or Off It?

The third read is your opponents’ position. If both of them are already at the kitchen line, a drive has to beat two blockers with almost no time to react, so the read leans drop. If either one is still off the line, in the transition zone or hanging back, a drive at that player’s feet leans drive.

#### What to look for

Look up before you hit, not after. Specifically look at the returner, because that’s the player who has to cover the most ground. Are they still moving forward? Did they stop at midcourt? A returner who hit and stood there is the best drive target in the game.

#### Why it matters

This is the read everyone teaches, and it’s the right one, but on its own it’s incomplete. Two players at the line with a deep, high ball in front of you is still a drive most of the time. One player off the line with a low, short ball is still a drop. Position tells you where the space is. Depth and height tell you whether you can actually hit it there.

### The Two-of-Three Rule

When all three reads agree, the decision is made for you. When they don’t, two out of three decides. Deep, high, and off the line is a drive. Short, low, and on the line is a drop. Anything mixed: count the reads, and go with the majority. The point isn’t which shot you prefer. It’s that you ran the checklist.

#### Four examples from a rec game

- Deep return, bounces high, returner still at midcourt. Three for drive. Hit it at the feet of the player who’s moving. This is the easiest point you’ll win all day and 3.5 players drop it out of habit.

- Short return, bounces low, both at the line. Three for drop. Step in, take it early, drop it into the kitchen, follow it in. Driving here is how you end up with a ball at your shoelaces.

- Deep return, bounces low, both at the line. One for drive (depth), two for drop (height, position). Drop it. This is the one that fools drivers, because it’s deep and “feels” like a drive. It isn’t.

- Short return, bounces high, returner off the line. One for drop (depth), two for drive (height, position). Drive it, at the player who’s moving. This is the one that fools droppers, because they’re close to the net and want to be gentle. Don’t be.

Self-check: in your next rec game, say the three reads out loud (or in your head) before every third shot: deep or short, high or low, on or off. If you can’t answer all three before contact, you’re still guessing. That’s the leak.

### Drive, Then Drop: The Fifth Shot

A drive doesn’t have to win the point. Its job is to pull a weak ball out of your opponents so the fifth shot is an easy drop. Drive the third, read the block, drop the fifth, get to the line. This drive-and-drop sequence is how 4.0 players use the drive, and it’s why their drives look more effective than yours even when they aren’t hit harder.

#### How it works

When your drive is blocked back, it usually comes back softer and shorter than the original return. That’s a short, often low ball with your opponents now set at the line: two, sometimes three reads for drop. So you drop it. You didn’t change your mind. The reads changed, and you ran the checklist again.

#### The mistake

Driving the fifth because you drove the third. The block comes back, you’re fired up, you hit it again, and it goes into the net or gets counter-punched. Every ball gets its own three reads. The third shot doesn’t get a vote on the fifth.

### The Drill: 10 Minutes, Three Stages

Partner at the kitchen line feeding returns to you at the baseline. You call the read out loud before every ball, then hit the shot the checklist says. Ten balls per stage, count how many you called correctly and how many you executed. The goal is not perfect shots. The goal is a correct call on ten out of ten.

- Stage 1: one read at a time (10 balls). Your partner feeds deep or short on purpose and tells you which. You just call “deep” or “short” and hit the matching shot. Then do the same with high and low. You’re building the vocabulary.

- Stage 2: all three reads, partner sets the position (10 balls). Your partner feeds randomly and either stays at the line or backs up to midcourt before each feed. You call all three reads out loud, then hit. Count correct calls out of ten.

- Stage 3: live third-and-fifth (10 balls). Your partner feeds, you hit the third, they block it back, you hit the fifth, then follow it to the line. Two decisions per rally, both called out loud. This is the one that transfers to a real game.

Two sessions a week for a month. If your correct-call count isn’t at nine or ten out of ten by then, the problem is the reads, not the shots, and that’s something I can see on film in about two rallies.

Self-check: film ten of your third shots in a real game. For each one, pause before contact and score the three reads yourself. If your shot matched the checklist on fewer than seven of ten, run the drill before you work on anything else.

### Questions

Should I drive or drop my third shot in pickleball?

Read three things: depth of the return, height of the bounce, and whether your opponents are on the kitchen line or off it. Deep, high, and opponents off the line leans drive. Short, low, and opponents at the line leans drop. When the reads disagree, two out of three decides.

Why do my third shot drives keep getting blocked back at my feet?

Usually because you drove a ball you contacted below net height, or you drove into two players who were already set at the line. A lifted drive is the easiest ball in pickleball to block. Check the height and position reads before you swing; if either one says drop, drop.

Why do my third shot drops keep popping up?

Most often because you tried to drop a deep, high ball from behind the baseline, which is a long, hard drop, or because your feet were still moving at contact. A deep, high ball with a player off the line is a drive. Save the drop for short and low balls, and get your feet set before you hit it.

What is the drive-and-drop sequence in pickleball?

Drive the third shot to force a weak block, then drop the fifth shot into the kitchen and follow it to the line. The drive is not trying to win the point; it is setting up an easier drop. Each shot still gets its own three reads.

How do I practice the third shot decision, not just the shot?

Have a partner feed returns from the kitchen line while you call the three reads out loud before every ball, then hit what the checklist says. Score correct calls out of ten, not clean shots. Progress from single reads, to all three with your partner changing position, to live third-and-fifth rallies.
