How to Choose a Pickleball Coach in Daytona Beach

Volusia County has more coaching options than it did two years ago, and most of them are good at what they do. The mistake I see is not picking a bad coach; it is picking the wrong kind of coaching for where you are. Five questions sort it out.

1. What is your level, honestly?

Under 3.0: you need reps, rules, and a friendly group more than diagnosis. Take a class. 3.0 to 3.5 and stuck: you need someone to watch you play and tell you the one thing that is actually costing you points. That is a private lesson. 4.0 and up: you need hitting partners who are better than you and a coach who has played at that level.

2. What is the goal?

"Get better" is not a goal a coach can work with. "Stop losing the transition zone," "beat the 4.0s I play with on Tuesday," or "get through a tournament without my knee flaring up" are. The more specific your goal, the easier it is to match the coach.

3. Where will you actually play?

A club membership is worth it if you will drive there three times a week. If you play at public courts near home, a coach who comes to those courts saves you the drive and teaches you on the surface you compete on.

4. Private or group?

Group clinics are cheaper per person and better for beginners. Private lessons find problems clinics cannot. Semi-private (two or three players who already play together) is the underrated middle: you split the cost and the coach sees your real doubles patterns.

5. What does the coach actually look at?

Ask. Some coaches teach strokes. Some teach strategy and point construction. Some, like me, look at the whole chain, from shot selection down to how your hips and feet are set at contact, because I am a chiropractor as well as a coach. None of these is wrong; they suit different problems.

Which Coach for Which Player

  • Brand new, or under 3.0: Pictona PB101 with Mary Burns, or a city program at Nova. Get reps and friends first.
  • 3.0 to 3.5 and improving on your own: keep playing, add one clinic a week, and take the free 15-question diagnosis to find out which part of your game is leaking before you pay anyone.
  • 3.5 and stuck for six months or more: this is the player I built my practice for. One private lesson usually finds the leak; a four-pack fixes it. Details and booking.
  • 3.5 and stuck, with a body that hurts: also me, because the knee or the hip is often why the shot breaks down under pressure. See the knee-pain guide.
  • 4.0 and up, wanting better competition: Doug Simmons at Pictona, plus Pictona's leagues.
  • Any level, wants strategy and fitness together: Rhavie Marino.
  • New Smyrna or Edgewater: OMG for groups; I teach at Pettis Park and Phil Hall Park in New Smyrna Beach during non-peak hours.

Should I take private lessons or group clinics?

If you are new to the game or under about 3.0, a beginner class or clinic is the better value: you need volume and a friendly environment more than diagnosis. Once you are a consistent 3.0 to 3.5, private lessons pay off because the thing holding you back is usually one specific pattern that a coach has to see in your game, and a clinic will not find it.

How many lessons does it take to go from 3.5 to 4.0 in pickleball?

It depends on how much you play between lessons, but most 3.5 players do not need a long course of lessons. They need one or two sessions to find the leak, a drill they can run twice a week, and a check-in a few weeks later to confirm it stuck. A four-lesson package spread over six to eight weeks is a realistic starting point. Players who only take lessons and never drill between them take much longer.
