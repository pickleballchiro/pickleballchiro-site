### What This Diagnosis Covers

Fifteen questions across the five areas where 3.0–4.0 players leak the most points. Your answers are scored by category; the lowest-scoring one is the specific leak capping your rating right now.

#### First Four Shots

The serve, the return, the third shot, and the fourth. These four shots decide who reaches the kitchen line first, and most points between 3.0 and 4.0 players are effectively over before anyone gets there.

- When you serve, what usually happens?

- On your return of serve, where does the ball usually end up?

- Your third shot, the one that’s supposed to get you to the kitchen — what happens most often?

#### Shot Selection

Choosing the right shot for the ball you actually got: drive, drop, reset, or dink. A 3.5 player usually hits the shot they like; a 4.0 player hits the shot the ball height and position call for.

- When a ball comes at you below your waist, what’s your instinct?

- After you reset or dink, what usually happens next?

- How often do you feel like you “didn’t mean to hit it that hard,” or get caught off guard by your own shot?

#### Transition Zone

The space between the baseline and the kitchen line, roughly 7 to 15 feet from the net. Getting through it safely after the third shot, without getting stuck or rushing, is the single most common gap in a 3.5 game.

- After your third shot, what do you usually do?

- When a ball comes fast at your feet while you’re moving forward, what happens?

- How often do you feel like you’re “a step behind” during a point?

#### Kitchen / Soft Game

Dinking, resets, and handling speed-ups at the non-volley line. Points at the 4.0 level are won here, so a player who can’t sustain a dink rally or defend a speed-up can’t hold a lead against better opponents.

- Honest check: in a typical rec game, how often do you actually get to the kitchen line before the point is decided?

- A speed-up comes at you at the kitchen line — what usually happens?

- When you’re in a dink rally, what’s actually happening?

#### Physical

Whether your body can hold up your technique for a full session. Fatigue, stiffness, and old injuries change footwork and contact point in game three even when the mechanics looked fine in warmup. This is the part a coach who is also a chiropractor sees first.

- How does your game change between game one and game three of the same session?

- Do you do anything physical off the court (mobility, strength, conditioning) to support your game?

- Do you have any nagging stiffness, pain, or “that’s just how my body feels now” spots that affect how you move on court?
